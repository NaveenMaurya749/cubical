  \begin{abstract}
    We survey three foundational frameworks in modern type theory: Martin-L\"of Type Theory (MLTT), Homotopy Type Theory (HoTT), and Cubical Type Theory.
    We begin with MLTT as a deductive system based on dependent $\lambda$-calculus, emphasizing \emph{inductive types} and \emph{type formers}, including \emph{W-types}.
    We then present HoTT’s homotopical interpretation of types, where identity types correspond to path spaces, and discuss key principles such as univalence and higher inductive types. 
    Motivated by limitations in the computational behavior of HoTT, we introduce Cubical Type Theory via cubical sets,
    highlighting its improved properties, including \emph{computational univalence, canonicity,} and \emph{normalization}.
  \end{abstract}
